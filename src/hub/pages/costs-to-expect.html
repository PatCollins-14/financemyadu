Pat's guide lists the typical closing cost categories for HELOCs and home equity loans as appraisal fees, origination fees, title search or recording fees and other administrative costs. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains how a Loan Estimate organizes them:

  • Origination charges are upfront fees charged by your lender. Compare the totals across lenders.
  • Services you cannot shop for are required and chosen by the lender.
  • Services you can shop for may cost less if you shop for them separately. The lender should give you a list of approved providers.

Exact amounts depend on the lender, the loan and your property. This page does not list figures because only a lender's Loan Estimate can show yours.

Most lenders require an appraisal to determine your home's current market value. Pat's guide notes some lenders use an automated valuation model instead, which can speed things up and sometimes avoid an in-person appraisal. Ask which one applies and who pays.

Pat's guide lists these cost categories for a reverse mortgage: an appraisal fee, origination charges, closing costs, a counseling fee, and FHA mortgage insurance premiums, plus ongoing servicing fees, interest and a monthly mortgage insurance premium. It also notes that most upfront costs can be financed into the loan. Interest and fees are added to the balance over time, so the amount you owe goes up. See reverse mortgage for an ADU.

  • Property taxes. The City of Pasadena's ADU page notes an ADU will increase property taxes, set by Los Angeles County.
  • Insurance and upkeep. Ask your insurance agent how an ADU changes your policy. [PAT: whether to mention insurance as a cost category and how]

Design, permits and construction costs come from your builder, architect and city, not from the lender. Your lender can only tell you how much financing may be available and what it would cost to borrow. Get your builder's quote early so the two numbers can be compared.

  • Homeowners comparing offers who want to know what to look for on a Loan Estimate.
  • Homeowners looking for a number. No page can predict your costs without your loan details.

Some costs can be paid at closing, and some can be rolled into a loan. Paying from your pocket reduces what you borrow but needs cash now. Rolling costs into a loan keeps cash free but adds to what you owe. Ask your lender to show both ways.

  • Please list every fee, including third-party fees, and tell me which I can shop for.
  • Which costs are due at closing and which can be added to the loan?
  • Will I owe any ongoing fees after closing?
