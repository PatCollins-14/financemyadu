A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is a reverse mortgage for homeowners 62 and older. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that with a reverse mortgage you are not required to make monthly mortgage payments, but the amount you owe goes up over time because interest and fees are added to the balance. Our guide notes that proceeds can be taken as a lump sum, a line of credit, monthly payments or a combination, and that an existing mortgage is paid off first.

  • Pay your property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA or other required fees on time.
  • Keep the home in good condition.
  • Live in the home as your primary residence.
If you do not meet these obligations, the loan can become due, and you could risk foreclosure. Our guide also notes that you must complete counseling with a HUD-approved counselor before the loan.

Whether a reverse mortgage can fund an ADU, and how an ADU is treated, are questions this page does not answer. Our guide says a reverse mortgage is for a primary residence and that the loan could become due if you move out for long periods or convert the home into a rental. How renting an ADU on the property fits those rules is something to ask a reverse mortgage specialist directly.
