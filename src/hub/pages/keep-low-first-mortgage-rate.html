Your first mortgage is the largest loan you have. Its rate and payment shape your monthly budget for years. If its terms work well for you, replacing it deserves a hard look. Pat's refinance guide says that if you already have a low interest rate, refinancing your first mortgage may not be ideal, and a HELOC or home equity loan can be an alternative.

The reverse is also true. If your current mortgage is not working for you, a refinance may improve more than your ADU funding. The choice depends on your own numbers. Pat's guide says a refinance can make sense when the new loan improves cash flow, interest cost, loan term, payment stability or access to equity enough to justify the closing costs.

  1. Write down your current rate, term and balance. [PAT: whether to suggest clients bring their latest mortgage statement to the first call]
  2. Ask each lender how it treats that loan under each option.
  3. Compare the total cost of each path over the years you expect to keep the loan, using Loan Estimates.
  • Homeowners with a first mortgage they are happy with who are weighing a second loan.
  • Homeowners whose first mortgage no longer suits them and who are open to replacing it.
  • Homeowners who cannot look at their current loan terms. Without them, no honest comparison is possible.

Keeping your first mortgage protects its terms, but a second loan adds another payment and its own costs. Replacing your first mortgage can simplify things into one loan, but you give up the old terms and pay closing costs. Neither is free of trade-offs.

  • Under this option, what happens to my current first mortgage?
  • What would my new total monthly obligations be under each option?
  • What are the total closing costs under each option?
  • If my current loan has features I value, would I lose them?
