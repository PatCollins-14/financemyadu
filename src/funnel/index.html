Your options

Which option fits you?

There is no single ADU loan. These are the main ways homeowners pay for an ADU. Which one fits depends on your equity, your current mortgage, your age and your goal.

Second loan

HELOC or home equity loan

You borrow against the equity in your home and keep your current first mortgage in place. A HELOC lets you draw money as you need it. A home equity loan gives you one lump sum.

Tends to fitHomeowners with equity who want to leave their first mortgage alone.
Main trade-offIt is a second loan secured by your home. HELOC rates usually change over time, and you must repay what you borrow.
Ask a lenderHow is my limit set? What happens when the draw period ends? What are the closing costs?
New first mortgage

Cash-out refinance

You replace your current mortgage with a new, larger one and receive the difference in cash. You get a new rate, term and payment.

Tends to fitHomeowners whose whole picture improves by replacing the first mortgage, not only the cash they receive.
Main trade-offIf you already have a low first-mortgage rate, replacing that loan may not be ideal. There are closing costs, and your balance resets higher.
Ask a lenderHow does the new loan compare with my current one over the time I plan to stay?
Learn about it

Renovation or construction loan

These loans are built around the work being done. Fannie Mae describes a renovation loan that can finance a new ADU when a homeowner buys or refinances a one-unit home.

Tends to fitHomeowners who are also buying or refinancing and want the project in one loan.
Ask a lenderHow are funds released during construction? What plans and contractor details do you need?

Homeowners 62+

Reverse mortgage (HECM)

A reverse mortgage lets homeowners 62 and older turn part of their home equity into funds. You must keep paying property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees, keep the home in good condition, and live there as your primary residence. If you do not, the loan can become due.

Rental income and value

How lenders look at an ADU

Some programs may consider projected rental income or the value of your property after the ADU is built. Some do not. The answer depends on the loan program, not on the ADU alone.

For example, Fannie Mae's published guide allows rental income from one existing ADU in certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, and limits how much of your qualifying income it can be. An ADU that is not built yet is a different question to ask your lender.

Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program.

Investor loans that qualify a rental property on its rent, such as the DSCR loans described on Pat's Non-QM page, are a different situation from an owner-occupied home with an ADU. Do not assume they apply to your project. [PAT: confirm which programs you offer that consider ADU rental income or after-ADU value]

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide, B3-3.8-02, read 2026-10-23. Lenders set their own rules and may differ.

Homeowners 62 and older

Using a reverse mortgage to help fund an ADU

A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is a reverse mortgage for homeowners 62 and older. Before you can get one, you must meet with a HUD-approved counselor so you understand the terms and obligations.

What you must keep doing

  • Pay your property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees on time.
  • Keep the home in good condition.
  • Live in the home as your primary residence.
If you do not meet these obligations, the loan can become due. A reverse mortgage balance grows over time as interest and fees are added, which reduces the equity left in your home.
