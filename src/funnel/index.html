Which option fits you?

There is no single ADU loan. These are the main ways homeowners pay for an ADU. The right one depends on your equity, your current mortgage, your age and your goal.

Second loan

HELOC or home equity loan

Your first mortgage stays in place.

How it works

You borrow against your equity. A HELOC lets you draw money as you need it. A home equity loan gives you one lump sum.

Main trade-off

It is a second loan secured by your home. HELOC rates usually change over time, and you must repay what you borrow.

Ask a lender

How is my limit set? What happens when the draw period ends? What are the closing costs?

New first mortgage

Cash-out refinance

Your first mortgage is replaced.

How it works

A new, larger mortgage pays off your current one and you receive the difference in cash, with a new rate, term and payment.

Main trade-off

If you already have a low first-mortgage rate, replacing that loan may not be ideal. There are closing costs, and your balance resets higher.

Ask a lender

How does the new loan compare with my current one over the time I plan to stay?

Renovations and builds

Renovation or construction loan

Built around the work being done.

How it works

Pat helps homeowners finance renovations. These loans are built around the work being done, and Fannie Mae describes one that can finance a new ADU when a homeowner buys or refinances a one-unit home.

Ask a lender

How are funds released during construction? What plans and contractor details do you need?

How lenders look at an ADU

Some programs may consider projected rental income or the value of your property after the ADU is built. Some do not. The answer depends on the loan program, not on the ADU alone.

For example, Fannie Mae's published guide allows rental income from one existing ADU in certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, and limits how much of your qualifying income it can be. An ADU that is not built yet is a different question to ask your lender.

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide, B3-3.8-02, read 2026-10-23. Lenders set their own rules and may differ.

Using a reverse mortgage to help fund an ADU

A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is a reverse mortgage for homeowners 62 and older. Before you can get one, you must meet with a HUD-approved counselor so you understand the terms and obligations.

What you must keep doing

  • Pay your property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees on time.
  • Keep the home in good condition.
  • Live in the home as your primary residence.

If you do not meet these obligations, the loan can become due. A reverse mortgage balance grows over time as interest and fees are added, which reduces the equity left in your home.
