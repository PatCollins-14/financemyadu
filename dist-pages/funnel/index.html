Which option fits you?

There is no single ADU loan. These are the main ways homeowners pay for an ADU. The right one depends on your equity, your current mortgage, your age and your goal.

Second loan

HELOC or home equity loan

Your first mortgage stays in place.

How it works

You borrow against your equity. A HELOC lets you draw money as you need it. A home equity loan gives you one lump sum.

Main trade-off

It is a second loan secured by your home. HELOC rates usually change over time, and you must repay what you borrow.

Ask a lender

How is my limit set? What happens when the draw period ends? What are the closing costs?

New first mortgage

Cash-out refinance

Your first mortgage is replaced.

How it works

A new, larger mortgage pays off your current one and you receive the difference in cash, with a new rate, term and payment.

Main trade-off

If you already have a low first-mortgage rate, replacing that loan may not be ideal. There are closing costs, and your balance resets higher.

Ask a lender

How does the new loan compare with my current one over the time I plan to stay?

Renovations and builds

Renovation or construction loan

Built around the work being done.

How it works

We help homeowners finance renovations. These loans are built around the work being done, and Fannie Mae describes one that can finance a new ADU when a homeowner buys or refinances a one-unit home.

Ask a lender

How are funds released during construction? What plans and contractor details do you need?

How lenders look at an ADU

Some programs may consider projected rental income or the value of your property after the ADU is built. Some do not. The answer depends on the loan program, not on the ADU alone.

For example, Fannie Mae's published guide allows rental income from one existing ADU in certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, and limits how much of your qualifying income it can be. An ADU that is not built yet is a different question to ask your lender.

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide, B3-3.8-02, read 2026-10-23. Lenders set their own rules and may differ.

Common questions about financing an ADU

Will I lose my low first-mortgage rate?
It depends on the option. A HELOC or home equity loan is a second loan, so your first mortgage stays in place. A cash-out refinance replaces your first mortgage with a new one, with a new rate, term and payment. Our own refinance guide notes that if you already have a low rate, refinancing the first mortgage may not be ideal, and a HELOC or home equity loan can be an alternative. We will compare the options for your numbers.
Can I finance a renovation along with my ADU?
We help homeowners finance renovations as well as ADUs. Some loans are built around the work being done, and Fannie Mae describes a renovation loan that can finance a new ADU when a homeowner buys or refinances a one-unit home. Whether an ADU and other renovation work can be in one loan depends on the program. [PAT: which of your programs can combine an ADU with other renovation work, and what they need]
Can rental income from an ADU count toward qualifying?
Some programs allow it in limited cases and some do not. For example, Fannie Mae's guide allows rental income from one existing ADU in certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, and caps how much of your qualifying income it can be. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program. [PAT: confirm which of your programs consider ADU rental income or after-ADU value]
How long until funds are available?
Timelines vary based on the loan, the property, the appraisal, your documents and underwriting. A complete application and quick replies help avoid delays. We explain the expected timeline at the start and keep you updated. [PAT: typical timelines for the ADU financing options you offer]

What documents will I need?
Most borrowers provide identification, income documentation, bank or asset statements, employment history, and information about current debts. Self-employed and alternative-income programs may require different documents. We give you a specific checklist for your loan type.
What factors affect my mortgage interest rate?
Your credit profile, loan type, property type, occupancy, down payment or equity, loan term, points and current market conditions can all affect pricing. The lowest advertised rate is not always the lowest total-cost option.
What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?
Pre-qualification is usually an early estimate based on information you provide. A pre-approval involves a deeper review of your credit, income, assets and debts. Because the information is verified, a pre-approval generally carries more weight with real estate agents and sellers.
