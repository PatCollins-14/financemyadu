HELOC vs. cash-out refinance for an ADU: which is better?

Last reviewed: October 23, 2026

Short answerNeither is better for everyone. A HELOC adds a second loan and leaves your first mortgage alone. A cash-out refinance replaces your first mortgage with a new, larger one. If your current first-mortgage terms work well for you, replacing them is a big trade-off. If the whole picture improves, a refinance can make sense. The right answer comes from comparing your actual numbers.

The core difference

HELOC (or home equity loan)Cash-out refinance
What happens to my first mortgage?It stays in place. The new loan is a second mortgage behind it.It is paid off and replaced by a new, larger first mortgage.
How do I get the money?A HELOC is a line you draw on. A home equity loan pays a lump sum.You receive the extra amount as cash at closing.
What changes about my payment?You add a second payment. A HELOC rate usually changes over time.You get a new rate, term and payment on the whole loan.
Main riskYour home secures the loan. The CFPB says you could lose your home if you cannot repay.Your home secures the loan, and the new balance is higher than the old one. Our guide notes you may extend your term and pay more interest in total.

Why your current rate matters

Our refinance guide makes this point: a cash-out refinance can be a useful tool, but if you already have a low interest rate, refinancing your first mortgage may not be ideal. In that case, a HELOC or home equity loan can be an alternative, because it leaves your first mortgage as it is.

That does not make the second loan the winner. A second loan has its own costs and, for a HELOC, a rate that usually changes. The only fair comparison is your whole financing cost over the time you plan to keep the loan. Ask each lender for a Loan Estimate so you can compare them side by side.

Questions that settle it

  • How long do I plan to stay in this home?
  • How do my current first-mortgage terms compare with what a new loan would offer?
  • Do I need the money all at once, or in stages as the ADU gets built?
  • How comfortable am I with a payment that can change?

Our guide says to work out the break-even point, which is how long it takes for a new loan's benefits to cover its closing costs, and compare that with how long you expect to stay. [PAT: how you walk clients through the comparison for an ADU project]

Who this fits

  • HELOC or home equity loan tends to fit homeowners who like their current first mortgage and have enough equity to borrow against.
  • Cash-out refinance tends to fit homeowners whose whole financing picture improves by replacing the first mortgage, not only the cash they receive.

Who this does not fit

  • A cash-out refinance may be a poor fit if you are happy with your current first-mortgage terms and the new loan would give those up for the ADU funds alone.
  • A HELOC may be a poor fit if you need a steady, fixed payment and a variable rate would worry you.

Trade-offs

Both put your home on the line, both have closing costs, and both change your monthly budget. The difference is what you keep and what you give up. A second loan keeps your first mortgage and adds to your obligations. A refinance resets your first mortgage and replaces its terms.

What to ask a lender

  • Can you show me both options side by side with a Loan Estimate?
  • What is the total cost of each over the number of years I expect to keep the loan?
  • Which one leaves me more flexibility if the ADU costs change?
