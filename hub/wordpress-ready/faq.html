Questions and answers

How do I finance an ADU in California?

There is no single ADU loan. Homeowners generally look at a few tools: a HELOC or home equity loan, a cash-out refinance, a renovation or construction loan, or, for homeowners 62 and older, a reverse mortgage. Which one fits depends on your equity, your current mortgage, your timeline and how you plan to use the ADU.

Read the full guide: How do I finance an ADU in California?

Can I use a HELOC to build an ADU?

A HELOC is one option homeowners consider for ADU costs. It is a line of credit secured by your home that you draw on as needed. It is also a second loan, the rate usually changes over time, and your home is the collateral.

Read the full guide: Can I use a HELOC to build an ADU?

Is a HELOC or a cash-out refinance better for an ADU?

Neither is better for everyone. A HELOC adds a second loan and leaves your first mortgage alone. A cash-out refinance replaces your first mortgage with a new, larger one. Comparing your actual numbers is the only way to decide.

Read the full guide: HELOC vs. cash-out refinance for an ADU: which is better?

How do construction and renovation loans work for an ADU?

These loans are built around the work being done. Fannie Mae describes a renovation loan that can finance adding an ADU when a homeowner buys or refinances a one-unit property, and a construction-to-permanent loan for a new home with an ADU. Which program fits depends on your project and your home.

Read the full guide: How do construction and renovation loans work for an ADU?

Can I finance a renovation along with my ADU?

We help homeowners finance renovations as well as ADUs. Whether an ADU and other renovation work can be in one loan depends on the program and the lender, so ask before you plan the budget around it.

Read the full guide: How do construction and renovation loans work for an ADU?

Can ADU rental income count toward qualifying for a loan?

Sometimes, under narrow rules, and not under every program. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide allows rental income from one existing ADU on certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, with a cap. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program.

Read the full guide: Can ADU rental income count toward qualifying for a loan?

Can a reverse mortgage fund an ADU?

It depends on the program and the project, so ask a specialist before you plan around it. A reverse mortgage (HECM) is for homeowners 62 and older, and you must keep paying property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees, keep the home in good condition and live there as your primary residence. If you do not, the loan can become due.

Read the full guide: Can a reverse mortgage fund an ADU?

Will financing an ADU affect my current mortgage rate?

It depends on how you finance it. A HELOC or home equity loan leaves your first mortgage in place. A cash-out refinance replaces it with a new loan at a new rate. Ask any lender what happens to your current loan.

Read the full guide: Will financing an ADU affect my current mortgage rate?

What should I ask a lender before financing an ADU?

Ask what each option does to your current mortgage, how the amount is decided, what every fee is, and what the lender needs about the project. Ask at least two lenders for a Loan Estimate so you can compare them.

Read the full guide: What should I ask a lender before financing an ADU?

What costs should I expect when financing an ADU?

Expect the cost of the loan, such as lender charges and third-party services like an appraisal, and the cost of owning a larger property, such as property taxes. The building cost comes from your builder, not your lender.

Read the full guide: What costs should I expect when financing an ADU?

What should I know about financing an ADU in Pasadena?

The City of Pasadena publishes its own ADU rules and process. For financing, its page raises two points to discuss with a lender: reduced fees can depend on an optional agreement that limits how the unit can be rented, and an ADU will increase property taxes.
