Short answerAsk what each option does to your current mortgage, how the amount is decided, what every fee is, what happens if your plans change, and what the lender needs to know about the project. Then ask at least two lenders for a Loan Estimate so you can compare them side by side. The checklist below covers the questions that matter most.

Compare lenders the same way

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau advises requesting multiple Loan Estimates from different lenders so you can compare and choose the loan that is right for you. Bring the same project and the same numbers to each one.

About the loan

  • Which of these do you offer: HELOC, home equity loan, cash-out refinance, renovation or construction loan, reverse mortgage? Which do you recommend for me and why?
  • Is this loan a second mortgage, or does it replace my current mortgage?
  • Is the rate fixed or does it change? What can change and when?
  • How is the amount I can borrow decided? How will you value my home?
  • What can make the lender freeze, reduce or call the loan?

About costs

  • What are all the fees, listed one by one? Which ones can I shop for myself?
  • Which fees are due at closing, and which are due later?
  • Is there a cost if I pay the loan off early or close the line?
  • What ongoing costs should I expect, such as servicing or insurance?

About the ADU

  • What do you need to know about the project and the builder, and when?
  • Does the ADU's permit status matter to you?
  • Can any rental income or the value of the finished ADU be considered? Under which program, and for an existing unit only? Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program.
  • If I use a city fee-reduction agreement that limits rent, how does that affect your answer?

About timing and plans

  • How long does each step take? Timelines vary by loan, property, appraisal, documents and underwriting, so ask for your own estimate. [PAT: your typical timeline wording for ADU financing]
  • What happens if the project costs more than planned or takes longer?
  • What happens if I want to sell or refinance later?

About the lender

  • Who is the lender of record, and who will service the loan?
  • Who will I talk to during the process, and how often will you update me?
  • Can you give me a written Loan Estimate to compare?

Who this fits

  • Homeowners who are early in their research and want a list to bring to every conversation.

Who this does not fit

  • Homeowners who have already compared Loan Estimates and chosen. This is a starting checklist, not a substitute for the paperwork you receive.

Trade-offs

Asking many questions takes time, and some answers will depend on documents you have not yet gathered. It is still cheaper to ask now than to find out at closing.

What to ask a lender

Start with the four questions that decide the most: what happens to my current mortgage, how the amount is set, what every fee is, and what the lender needs about the project.
