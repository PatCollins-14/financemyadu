Short answerThere is no single ADU loan. Homeowners generally look at a few tools: a home equity line of credit (HELOC) or home equity loan, a cash-out refinance, a renovation or construction loan, or, for homeowners 62 and older, a reverse mortgage. Which one fits depends on your equity, your current mortgage, your timeline and how you plan to use the ADU.

What counts as an ADU

An accessory dwelling unit (ADU) is a smaller living space on the same lot as a single-family home. Fannie Mae describes it as a space that includes living, sleeping, cooking and bathroom areas independent of the main home. It can be inside the home, such as a basement apartment, attached to it, such as a living area over a garage, or fully detached from the home.

Cities set their own rules for what you can build and how. See ADU financing in Pasadena for one example, and talk to your city's planning office before you plan the money.

Your main financing options at a glance

Fannie Mae states that there is no specific type of financing required for a home with an ADU. These are the common choices.

Four questions to answer first

  1. How much equity do you have? Equity is your home's value minus what you owe. Lenders confirm value with an appraisal or an automated valuation, so the number you estimate may not match theirs.
  2. What are the terms of your current first mortgage? This decides whether replacing it makes sense. See keeping your first-mortgage rate.
  3. What will the ADU do for you? Rental income, space for family, or added value each point to different choices. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program. See ADU rental income and qualifying.
  4. What is your timeline, and who is building? Lenders may ask for information about the project. Ask early. See questions to ask your lender.

What a mortgage professional does, and does not do

We help you understand the financing: the choices, the numbers and the trade-offs. Design, permits and construction are a different job. For those, talk to a licensed builder or architect and your city's planning office.

Who this fits

  • Homeowners who are still planning an ADU and want to understand the money before they hire anyone.
  • Homeowners with equity who want to compare a second loan with replacing their first mortgage.
  • Homeowners buying or refinancing who want to ask whether the project can be part of that loan.

Who this does not fit

  • Homeowners who have not yet confirmed that their property can have an ADU. Start with your city, then come back to the financing.
  • Anyone looking for design, permit or construction advice. That is outside a mortgage professional's expertise.

Trade-offs

Every option here uses your home as collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home. Beyond that, each option has its own cost: a second loan adds a payment on top of your first mortgage, a refinance changes your first mortgage, and a reverse mortgage reduces your equity over time. There are closing costs with most of them. See costs to expect.

What to ask a lender

  • Which of these options do you offer, and which do you recommend for my situation and why?
  • How will you estimate my home's value, and how does that set what I can borrow?
  • What will you need to know about the project and the builder?
  • Can I see a Loan Estimate so I can compare lenders side by side?
