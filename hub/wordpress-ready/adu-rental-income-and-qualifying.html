Short answerSometimes, under narrow rules, and not under every program. For example, Fannie Mae's Selling Guide allows rental income from one existing ADU on certain purchase and limited cash-out refinance loans, and caps how much of your qualifying income it can be. An ADU that is not built yet raises a different question for your lender. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program.

What one major guide says

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, topic B3-3.8-02, sets rules for ADU rental income. As of the version dated September 2, 2026, it says:

  • It applies to a one-unit ADU allowed with a one-unit primary residence.
  • It applies to purchase or limited cash-out refinance transactions only.
  • Rental income is allowed from only one existing ADU.
  • The qualifying rental income from the ADU is limited to 30% of the borrower's total qualifying income.
  • The lender must obtain a supporting rent form (Form 1007 or Form 1025) for the rent, as it would for other rental income on the subject property.

Fannie Mae's ADU page adds that a borrower buying or refinancing a home with an existing ADU who qualifies for a HomeReady loan can include rental income to help qualify.

This is one program's rule. Other programs and lenders may have different rules, and lenders can add their own. Rules change, so ask your lender to confirm the current version.

What about an ADU that is not built yet?

The rule above says "existing" ADU. If your ADU is still a plan, ask your lender directly whether any rental income or after-construction value can be considered, under which program, and what the lender would need to see. Local rules can limit rent

Some city programs tie reduced fees to agreements that limit how a unit can be rented. For example, the City of Pasadena's ADU page describes optional Housing Agreements that restrict use of the unit. Ask your lender how any such limit would affect rental income you hope to count. See ADU financing in Pasadena.

Who this fits

  • Homeowners buying or refinancing a home that already has a permitted ADU.
  • Homeowners who want to understand how a lender might view rent before they plan their budget around it.

Who this does not fit

  • Anyone counting on rent from an ADU that does not exist yet to qualify for the loan that would pay for it.
  • Anyone who needs to depend on rental income as a sure thing. Rent can change, stop or come in lower than you expect.

Trade-offs

Counting rent can help some borrowers qualify, but a lender may limit how much it counts, may require documents, and may treat the income as less certain than a paycheck. Planning your budget so you can handle the payment even without the rent is a safer way to think about it.

What to ask a lender

  • Does this program count rental income from an ADU at all, and is it an existing unit only?
  • Is there a cap on how much counts?
  • What documents prove the rent, and who orders them?
  • Does the unit's permit status matter?
  • If I use a city program with rent limits, how does that change the answer?
