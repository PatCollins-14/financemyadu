Short answerThe City of Pasadena publishes its own ADU rules and process, so start there for design and permit questions. For financing, the city's pages raise points worth raising with a lender: reduced fees can depend on an optional agreement that limits how the unit can be rented, and an ADU will increase property taxes. Everything local on this page comes from the city's own ADU page, read on October 23, 2026.

What the City of Pasadena says

This summary is from the City of Pasadena Planning & Community Development ADU page. The page shows no update date, so check it for the latest.

  • Current rules. The page says updated regulations for ADUs and Junior ADUs (JADUs) went into effect on May 20, 2024, under Ordinance No. 7420.
  • Applying. You can apply and submit plans online through the city's CSS Permit Portal, and a virtual consultation is available for preliminary zoning feedback before you submit plans.
  • Standard plans. The city's ADU Standard Plans Program offers pre-approved designs, from studios to two-bedroom units. The page says modifications to pre-approved plans are not allowed, however minor.
  • JADUs. The page describes a JADU as capped at 500 square feet and built within the existing or proposed single-family home or attached non-habitable space.

Fees and agreements: why a lender should hear about them

The city describes reduced fees for smaller ADUs and for ADUs that use a standard plan or an optional Housing Agreement. The page says reductions are not retroactive, multi-family sites do not qualify, and a Housing Agreement must be signed before the permit is issued.

The two optional agreements the page describes both restrict how the unit is used or rented for at least seven years. One commits the unit to family members or City rental assistance clients. The other requires renting to households under a set income level.

That matters for financing. If you hope rental income will help you qualify or help you afford the payment, ask your lender how such a limit would affect it. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program. See ADU rental income and qualifying.

Property taxes

The city's page notes that an ADU will increase your property taxes, and that Los Angeles County sets them, not the city. Build that into the monthly budget you plan with your lender. See costs to expect.

Who this fits

  • Pasadena homeowners planning an ADU who want to line up the permit side and the money side.
  • Homeowners who plan to use a standard plan or a city fee reduction and want to understand what it could mean for rent and financing.

Who this does not fit

  • Homeowners outside Pasadena. Every city has its own rules.
  • Anyone looking for permit or design advice. Use the city's contacts. The ADU page lists separate email contacts for zoning questions and for building codes and the permit process.

Trade-offs

City programs that lower fees can come with conditions that affect how you can use the unit. A lower up-front cost can mean fewer choices later. Read the agreement before you sign, and ask your lender how it fits your plan.

What to ask a lender

  • If I use a standard plan or a Housing Agreement, does that change how you view my project or my rental income?
  • How do you account for added property taxes in my budget?
  • Do you need the approved plans or permit before you can finalize a loan?
