What a reverse mortgage is

A Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) is a reverse mortgage for homeowners 62 and older. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that with a reverse mortgage you are not required to make monthly mortgage payments, but the amount you owe goes up over time because interest and fees are added to the balance. Our guide notes that proceeds can be taken as a lump sum, a line of credit, monthly payments or a combination, and that an existing mortgage is paid off first.

What you must keep doing

  • Pay your property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA or other required fees on time.
  • Keep the home in good condition.
  • Live in the home as your primary residence.
If you do not meet these obligations, the loan can become due, and you could risk foreclosure. Our guide also notes that you must complete counseling with a HUD-approved counselor before the loan.

What is not known yet

Whether a reverse mortgage can fund an ADU, and how an ADU is treated, are questions this page does not answer. Our guide says a reverse mortgage is for a primary residence and that the loan could become due if you move out for long periods or convert the home into a rental. How renting an ADU on the property fits those rules is something to ask a reverse mortgage specialist directly.

Who this fits

  • Homeowners 62 and older who plan to stay in the home as their primary residence and can keep up with taxes, insurance and upkeep.
  • Families exploring an ADU for a parent or for the homeowner's own later years who want to understand every option before choosing.

Who this does not fit

  • Homeowners under 62.
  • Anyone who may not be able to keep paying property taxes and insurance.
  • Anyone who wants to leave as much home equity as possible to heirs without understanding how the balance grows.

Trade-offs

  • The balance grows. Interest and fees are added over time, so the equity left in your home shrinks.
  • Costs. Our guide lists appraisal, origination, closing costs, a counseling fee, FHA mortgage insurance premiums, servicing fees and interest. See costs to expect.
  • Your heirs. Your home can still be left to heirs, but they will need to repay the balance to keep it, as with other mortgages.

What to ask a lender

  • Can proceeds be used for an ADU project, and what does the lender need to see?
  • How would an ADU on my property, and any rent from it, be treated under the loan rules?
  • What will my ongoing costs be, and how do I stay current on taxes and insurance?
  • What happens if my health or living situation changes?
  • Can I bring a family member to the required counseling session?
