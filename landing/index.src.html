The problem

Here's where most ADU plans stall

You want the ADU. You have a contractor in mind. Then the financing hits one of these walls.

You don't want to give up your rate

A cash-out refinance pays for the build, but it replaces the first mortgage you worked hard to get. For a lot of homeowners, that's a dealbreaker.

Your equity today doesn't cover it

A regular home equity line is capped by what your home is worth right now. The project costs more than that cap leaves you. So the plan sits.

You just bought, or you're about to

You found a home with room for an ADU, but you put little down. There's barely any equity yet, so a regular equity line has nothing to give you.

The fix: borrow against the finished home. This home equity line is sized on your home's value after the ADU is built. Same house, same first mortgage, a much bigger budget to work with.

How it works

A regular home equity line, plus one extra review

If you've ever had a HELOC, most of this will feel familiar. The difference is that the project gets reviewed too, and your home is appraised as if the work were already done.

What this is

An After-Renovation HELOC is a home equity line of credit that sits behind your first mortgage. It is sized on your home's after-renovation value: what the appraiser says your home will be worth once the ADU or remodel is finished.

  • Second loan. Your first mortgage is not touched.
  • Your income, credit and assets are reviewed the usual way.
  • A separate team reviews only the project: plans, bid and contractor.
  • No standard draw schedule and no draw inspections.
  • One final inspection to confirm the work is done.

  1. Talk it through with Pat

    Tell Pat about your home, your first mortgage and the ADU you want. You'll know quickly if this program fits or if another loan makes more sense.

  2. Your contractor sends the project details

    A detailed cost estimate, the plans or drawings, a copy of their license and insurance, and a short contractor questionnaire.

  3. The project gets reviewed

    A renovation team checks that the budget, scope and contractor make sense. They look at the project only, not your finances.

  4. Your home is appraised "as finished"

    The appraiser values your home as if the ADU were already built. That after-renovation value is what the line is based on.

  5. Your line closes and the build starts

    Your first mortgage stays put. There's project supervision after closing and one final inspection when the work is complete.

Questions people ask first

Will I lose my first mortgage?

No. This is a second loan that sits behind your first mortgage. There's no refinance, so your first mortgage, its rate and its payment stay exactly as they are.

How is this different from a regular HELOC?

A regular HELOC is based on what your home is worth today. This one is based on what your home will be worth after the project is finished. To get there, it adds two things: a review of the project, and an appraisal of your home "as finished."

Can I use my own contractor?

Yes. Your contractor gets reviewed: their license and insurance, a short questionnaire, the cost estimate and the plans. If they check out, they build it.

Are there draw inspections or a construction consultant?

No standard draw schedule, no draw-by-draw inspections and no construction consultant. There is project supervision after closing, which comes with its own fee, and one final inspection to confirm the work is done. Pat goes over every cost with you up front.

Can I finance a remodel along with my ADU?

Yes. Kitchens, bathrooms, additions, roofing, decks, pools and other improvements are eligible, on their own or alongside the ADU.

Who qualifies?

Owners of a primary home or a second home, on a single-family or two‑unit property. You'll need a solid credit profile and fully documented income. Investment properties don't qualify for this program. If yours is a rental, Pat can walk you through other options.

I'm buying a home. Can I use this right after closing?

Yes. There's no waiting period after the purchase. You can buy the home, then set up the line based on its finished value and start the project.

Will rental income from the new ADU help me qualify?

Plan on qualifying with the income you have today, not on future rent from the new ADU. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program. Pat will tell you how your situation is treated.

What about the rate and costs?

Those depend on your credit, your home and the size of the line, so they're covered one on one. Pat lays them out before you decide anything.
