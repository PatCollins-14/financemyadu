What it is

A HELOC you already understand, sized on the finished home

You borrow against your home's after-renovation value: what the appraiser says it will be worth once the ADU is done.

  • Second lien. It sits behind your first mortgage. No refinance.
  • A line of credit. It works like the HELOCs you've heard of.
  • No standard draws. No draw-by-draw inspections and no construction consultant.
  • One final inspection to confirm the work is done, with project supervision after closing.

1The lender

  • Takes your application and issues the approval
  • Reviews your income, assets and credit
  • Closes and funds your line of credit

2The renovation team

  • Reviews only the project: budget, plans and contractor
  • Supervises the project after closing

Three example properties, worked out line by line

Same math the program uses. Each example puts a standard HELOC on today's value next to the After‑Renovation HELOC on the finished value, so you can see exactly where the difference comes from.

Standard HELOC

Today's value × the lender's limit (80% to 85% in these examples), minus your first mortgage.

After‑Renovation HELOC

The lesser of 95% of the finished value or 125% of today's value, minus your first mortgage.

How it works

From application to building in five steps

  1. Start your application

    Click Get Pre-qualified and fill out the secure online application. Pat follows up to go over your home, your first mortgage and the ADU you want.

  2. Your contractor sends the project details

    The cost estimate, the plans or drawings, their license and insurance, and a short questionnaire.

  3. The project gets reviewed

    The renovation team checks the budget, scope and contractor. A feasibility review, not a second look at your finances.

  4. Your home is appraised as finished

    The appraiser values your home as if the ADU were already built. That's the value your line is based on.

  5. Close and build

    Your line closes, your first mortgage stays put, and the work starts. One final inspection when it's done.

Straight answers

Questions people ask first

Will I lose my first mortgage?

No. This is a second loan that sits behind your first mortgage. There's no refinance, so your first mortgage, its rate and its payment stay exactly as they are.

How is this different from a regular HELOC?

A regular HELOC is based on what your home is worth today. This one is based on what your home will be worth after the project is finished. To get there, it adds two things: a review of the project, and an appraisal of your home "as finished."

What happens when I click Get Pre-qualified?

It opens Pat Collins' secure online application with E Mortgage Capital. Fill it out and Pat follows up to go over your home, your project and your options. Starting an application is not a loan approval.

Can I use my own contractor?

Yes. Your contractor gets reviewed: their license and insurance, a short questionnaire, the cost estimate and the plans. If they check out, they build it.

Are there draw inspections or a construction consultant?

No standard draw schedule, no draw-by-draw inspections and no construction consultant. There is project supervision after closing, which comes with its own fee, and one final inspection to confirm the work is done. Pat goes over every cost with you up front.

I'm buying a home. Can I use this right after closing?

Yes. There's no waiting period after the purchase. Buy the home, then set up the line based on its finished value and start the project.

Can I use it on a rental property?

No. This program is for primary homes and second homes, on single-family or two‑unit properties. If yours is a rental, Pat can walk you through other options.

Will rental income from the new ADU help me qualify?

Plan on qualifying with the income you have today, not on future rent from the new ADU. Rental income is not guaranteed and may not count toward qualification under every program. Pat will tell you how your situation is treated.

What about the rate and costs?

Those depend on your credit, your home and the size of the line, so they're covered one on one. Pat lays them out before you decide anything.
